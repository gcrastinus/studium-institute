When Expression Is Not Yet Speech

Expression on its own, even passionate self-expression, is not an art. A shout, for example, expresses something, but it does not yet say anything; it may have some significance in context, but it does not have meaning in the proper sense. A style can be vivid and still fail to assert anything or to move an audience toward what we want them to see to be true. Eagerness and passion are not an argument, and they are not persuasive in themselves; passion is a feeling, and it must have something that we are passionate about in order to have meaning and direction. So, the student who has been told to “be himself” and speak in his own voice may produce a recognizable tone, and may even say something enjoyable or troubling to hear, and still not have said anything that another person could clearly agree with, deny, or obey.

When “find your voice” or “express yourself” comes before adequate care for our words, the student is asked to be interesting before he can be clear, and to be sincere before he can be responsible for asserting or denying a claim. Authenticity then becomes something that an audience either feels in response to us as persons or does not; if they do not feel it, we can scarcely force them to; and the result is a breakdown of discourse, since our views on things become more a matter of personal response to someone else and less a matter of truth. If the only test of speech is that it came from the speaker, then no one can ever say that the speaker was wrong, unclear, or unjust, but only that he was not himself, which is a hard charge to sustain and one that can even seem disrespectful to make.

These problems arise as much for the listener or reader as for the speaker or writer. If we do not know what another person is trying to say, that can sometimes be our own fault; but it is also incumbent on the speaker, since he is the one with something to say, to make sure that others are able to understand it. Speech is ultimately public. A promise, a prayer, an argument, a verdict, and a diagnosis are all acts that require someone else to know what was said; and a community that cannot tell what was promised, prayed, argued, or decided is not yet a community, but only a collection of personal passions and feelings.

The speaker, too, will have difficulty if he cares only for his own voice and expression. A voice that cannot name, assert, or follow or give an argument will not become more capable by being left alone. It will only become more accustomed to being unanswerable; and so it will become less and less able to persuade, and therefore less and less effective.
