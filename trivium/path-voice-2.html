When expression is not yet speech

❧

Expression on its own, even passionate self-expression, is not an art. A shout expresses, but it does not yet say anything; it may have some significance in context, but it does not have meaning in the proper sense. A style can be vivid and still fail to express, to assert, or to move a listener toward what we want him to see to be true. Eagerness and passion are not an argument, and they are not persuasive in themselves. Passion in itself is nothing more than feeling, since passion must have something that we are passionate about in order to have meaning and direction. The student who has been told to “be himself” and speak in his own voice may produce a recognizable tone, and may even say something enjoyable or troubling to hear, and still not have said anything that another person could clearly agree with, deny, or obey.

When “find your voice” or “express yourself” comes before adequate care for our words, the student ends up being asked to be interesting before he can even be clear, and to be sincere before he can be responsible for asserting or denying a claim. Authenticity then becomes something a hearer either feels in response to us as persons or does not; if he does not feel it, we can scarcely force him to; and the result is a breakdown of discourse, since our views and perspectives on things become more a matter of personal response to someone else and less a matter of truth. If the only test of speech is that it came from the speaker, then no hearer can ever say that he was wrong, unclear, or unjust, but only that he was not himself, which is a hard charge to sustain and one that can even feel disrespectful to make.

The problems in these cases arise as much for the listener or reader as for the speaker or writer. If we genuinely do not know what another person is trying to say, that can sometimes be our own fault; but it is also incumbent on the speaker, since he is the one with something to say, to make sure that others are able to hear it. Speech is ultimately public. A promise, a prayer, an argument, a verdict, a diagnosis: these are acts that require someone else to know what was said. A community that cannot tell what was promised, prayed, argued, or enacted is not a community; it is a clamor of passions and feelings that happen to be personal.

The speaker too will have trouble if he cares only for his own voice and expression. A voice that cannot name, assert, or follow or give an argument will not grow stronger by being left alone. It will only grow more used to being unanswerable, and so it will become less and less able to persuade, and therefore less and less powerful.
