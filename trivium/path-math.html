What Is True in Starting from Mathematics

Many students would prefer to begin an education or even just an inquiry with mathematics and mathematical representations, and there are good reasons for this. The problems and questions that can be posed and answered using mathematical methods and results range from the elementary problems of calculation and geometry to higher algebra, group theory, and probability and statistics and these have very wide application across seemingly every domain. There is also no doubt of the expressive power of mathematics to capture things like quantity and structure and other formal considerations, either in themselves, when we consider pure mathematics, or as they can be found in real things.

It cannot be doubted, therefore, that mathematics is a powerful and even essential tool for understanding the world, and not just at the level of practical engineering but at the level of the most fundamental truths about nature and time and motion. In the late thirteenth century, Roger Bacon said that the gateway and the key to the study of nature is mathematics; this follows Aristotle himself, who had said that quantity is the first property of material substance; and this came increasingly to be recognized and exploited for studying the world. Mathematics, therefore, has a central place among the sciences, and it receives a special honor in the late modern world.

The increasing importance of mathematics and its profitable application in so many fields led many educators to turn their attention toward mathematics and away from the more traditional studies of language and logic or even to think somehow that language study is opposed to a mathematical perspective. But there is in principle no opposition between the study of discourse and the study of mathematics and natural science. More than that, they are clearly complementary, which is why they are always supposed to be included in every effort to give students a general education.

So, what is true in starting from mathematics is that mathematics is extremely powerful for understanding the world. What has not yet been shown is that mathematics is more general or more expressively powerful than language. The following pages lay out the reasons why it is not.
