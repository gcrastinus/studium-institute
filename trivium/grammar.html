The first art

Grammar

❧

Grammar is the art of speaking and writing correctly. It concerns significant speech, that is, speech that signifies something, and the arrangement of its parts, so that what we mean can actually be said.

What grammar studies

Grammar treats the word as a conventional sign and asks how such signs are put together in a given language. It studies letters and sounds, the parts of speech (noun, verb, and the rest), and how words are joined into sentences. A cough is a vocal sound but not a word, and a cry signifies something but not by convention. A name is not yet a sentence, and a sentence is not yet an argument.

Grammar and logic

A sentence can be grammatically correct and false, and it can be incorrect and true. Walshe gives a pair of examples: the grammarian judges “man is stone” correct and “man are animal” incorrect, while the logician judges the first false and the second true, though badly expressed (Commentary on the Categories, “What is Logic About?”). So grammar asks whether something is correctly said; logic asks whether what is said is so. Both questions are needed, and neither replaces the other.

For the same reason, the parts of speech are not the same as the categories. “Man” and “humanity” differ in grammatical form, but both fall under the category of substance. “Blue triangle” and “equilateral triangle” have the same grammatical form, adjective and noun, but the first joins an accident to its subject and the second joins a difference to its genus. Grammar and logic consider the same words in different ways.

Why grammar comes first

Logic works on statements, and a statement is made of words. Rhetoric offers speech to a hearer, and there is nothing to offer if nothing has been said. So, the classical tradition begins with grammar: the words must be correctly joined before anything can follow logically from them or be offered to a hearer. A single word, such as “white,” signifies but is neither true nor false; only when words are composed into a statement can what is said be true or false.
