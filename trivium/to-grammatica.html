Practicing grammar

❧

The account of grammar is completed by practice in the set of exercises, Ars Grammatica. It begins with the opening study, What Grammar Is, which the set of exercises marks “start here.” That study is not scored, but it should not be skipped; it sets out what the art is for and how the studies that follow are ordered. The next study is What Grammar Is For, and the rest follow in the order of the set of exercises.

When to return

A first pass is finished when we can state the following in our own words:

  • What grammar asks that logic and rhetoric do not.
  • Why man is stone is grammatically correct but false, and man are animal incorrect but true.
  • Why a correctly formed sentence is not yet a true one.

Then we go on to Logic. We return to grammar, and to Ars Grammatica, whenever the terms become unclear, that is, whenever we cannot tell what is being said of what. That is the ordinary course of learning an art: an account, then practice, then the account again with better questions.
