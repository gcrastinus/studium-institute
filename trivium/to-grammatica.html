Bridge

Go and practice grammar

❧

The last page said what this art is for. The application will make you do it. Open Ars Grammatica and complete the opening study, What Grammar Is — the unscored preliminary the app itself marks “start here.” That deck is an orientation: grammar as an art that frees you to say what you mean, well-formed against true, from voice to the complete saying. Then continue in the app’s own order. The next study in that order is What Grammar Is For. Do not skip the preliminary because it is not scored.

Return to this course when you can say, in your own words, what grammar asks that logic and rhetoric do not. A good test is Walshe’s pair. If you can tell why “man is stone” is a grammatical success and a logical failure, and why “man are animal” is the reverse, you have the distinction this lesson needed. The app will then take you into the parts and magnitudes of speech. This walk-through will not duplicate those drills.

Begin at the study deck What Grammar Is, which the app marks “start here.” Live address: grammatica.studiuminstitute.org.

Open Ars Grammatica practice

Default recommendation: finish a first pass of grammar before logic. Detour to logic first only if you cannot tell a statement from a question or a command. Detour to rhetoric first only if you are already trapped in empty debate and need to see what persuasion is for — and even then, come back. When terms go muddy later, return here and to the app. The cycle this course intends is ordinary: doctrine, then practice, then doctrine again with better questions.

When you come back

You are the one who decides when a first pass is finished; nothing here is a test. But there is a way to tell, and it is not how many decks you cleared. Try to say these out loud, in your own words, without opening anything:

  • What grammar asks that logic and rhetoric do not.
  • Why man is stone is a grammatical success and a logical failure, and man are animal the reverse.
  • Why a well-formed sentence is not yet a true one — and where truth does arrive.

If those come, you have what this lesson was for. If the second one will not come, that is the one to go back for; it is the hinge of the whole trivium.

Welcome back

Then the first art has had its first pass, and you are in the part of this course that most students never reach: the return. Grammar is not finished — an art is not finished — but it has been begun in the only way an art can be, by doing it.

Go on to Logic. You will find that the second art assumes exactly what you have just been practising: that there are terms which mean, and sayings which are composed. Logic will ask what follows from them.

Come back to grammar, and to Ars Grammatica, whenever your terms go muddy in the middle of something else — when you cannot tell what is being said of what. That is not a relapse. That is the cycle working.
