Introduction to the Trivium

❧

This interactive overview is for a motivated student or an interested adult who wants to understand the foundations of the classical trivium (which is the coordinated studies of grammar, logic, and rhetoric), in particular why the classical tradition begins all inquiry from words and takes words as its fundamental instrument.

Contemporary studies often want to begin from things, from mathematical representations, from methods, from “critical thinking,” or from the felt need for the student to find how to express himself and engage with the world.

But the classical tradition of Plato, Aristotle, St. Augustine, St. Thomas Aquinas begins from speech. Human teaching and learning are fundamentally linguistic; and so that is where the tradition begins. Grammar, logic, and rhetoric are arts of language (or, to use the more proper traditional term, arts of speech), ordered to truth and to persuasion in a community of speakers.

Work through the modules in the order given. That is the ideal.

But if you already have a strong objection or a strong presupposition that we should not begin with words (that science should come first, or that logic puzzles are enough), start at Modern starting-points and follow that path until it returns here.

Ideally, you would have a teacher to lead you through these studies; but in our time it is often not easy to find a teacher; and this is especially true for older students.

The three sets of exercises

This interactive overview is expository. It does not replace practice. At the proper places this course turns to three sets of exercises now available, which train the arts rather than describe them. Those sets of exercises do not replace study of the classic texts.

Ars Grammatica grammar · practice Ars Syllogistica logic · practice Ars Rhetorica rhetoric · practice

The right order to proceed through the exposition is to begin with Grammar, then move to Logic, then, finally, Rhetoric. Sometimes, an excursus may be warranted, to answer objections, consider other perspectives, and in general to solidify your understanding.

The quadrivium (the arts that begin from quantity, arithmetic, music, geometry, and astronomy) is discussed at the end of this introduction but has its own course of study.
