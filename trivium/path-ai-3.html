Why the arts remain yours

❧

Grammar, logic, and rhetoric remain the student’s because the student remains the one who must understand, judge, and speak. A machine can supply wording. It cannot be the one taught, and it cannot stand in the community that teaching makes. Education is not the production of pages. It is the formation of a speaker who can be answerable for what is said.

If you came here because writing now seems optional, the foundation pages will treat that appearance as the thing to be examined, not as a verdict. The question is not whether a tool can draft. The question is whether you can still mean, follow, and offer — with the tool or without it. Those are the arts. They do not become less necessary when help gets faster. They become the only thing left that is yours.

The practice applications that follow are not there to compete with generators in speed. They are there so that you can still do, slowly and in your own person, what a liberal education has always asked of speech: join words so that something is said, order statements so that something follows, and speak so that a hearer can be shown.

The next pages begin from experiences no machine can have for you: being misunderstood, teaching a child a name, making a promise. If those still matter, the arts still matter.
