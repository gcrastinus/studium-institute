Practicing Logic

❧

In the set of exercises, Ars Syllogistica, a student can learn many aspects of logic as a liberal art.

Ars Syllogistica teaches logic as the art of reasoning well. It follows Aristotle and St. Thomas and is arranged by the three acts of the mind.

The first act, simple apprehension, covers the predicables, the categories, definition, and division. The second act, judgment, covers propositions and their opposition on the square, immediate inference, modal propositions, and diagramming propositions. The third act, reasoning, covers the syllogism. Students practice drawing conclusions and diagramming syllogisms, study syllogistic form in the abstract and in real examples, and separate an argument’s matter from its form. They go on to hypothetical, conjunctive, disjunctive, and modal syllogisms, mixed consequences, the enthymeme, chains of syllogisms, and arguments in ordinary speech.

The set of exercises ends with the other uses of reasoning: demonstration, dialectic, rhetoric, and spotting fallacies.

It begins with the opening study, What Is Logic · An Orientation, which the set of exercises marks “start here,” and then the First Act of the Mind · Simple Apprehension: the Predicables, the Categories, Division, and Definition. The Second Act (the proposition) and the Third Act (reasoning) follow. The syllogism comes last because it depends on the first two acts.
