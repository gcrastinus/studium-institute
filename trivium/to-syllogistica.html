Practicing Logic

❧

The account of logic is completed by practice in the set of exercises, Ars Syllogistica. It begins with the opening study, What Is Logic · An Orientation, which the set of exercises marks “start here,” and then the First Act of the Mind · Simple Apprehension: the Predicables, the Categories, Division, and Definition. The Second Act (the proposition) and the Third Act (reasoning) follow. The syllogism comes last because it depends on the first two acts.

If the terms will not stay distinct, the set of exercises also offers a preliminary card, The Art of Grammar. Tracking on the Progress page is optional; nothing is stored until it is turned on, and nothing leaves the browser.

When to return

A first pass is finished when we can state the following in our own words:

  • The three acts of the mind, in order, and what each forms: a term, a proposition, an argument.
  • Why a single term is neither true nor false.
  • The difference between a contradiction and a disagreement.
  • What makes a fallacy: it appears to conclude but does not.

Then we go on to Rhetoric. We return to logic whenever something looks like an argument and we cannot say whether anything follows; and if we cannot tell what is being said of what, we return first to grammar.
