Practicing rhetoric

❧

The account of rhetoric is completed by practice in the set of exercises, Ars Rhetorica. It begins at Act I, What Rhetoric Is, with the opening card The counterpart of dialectic, which is not scored. That study presents the definition, the three means of persuasion (ethos, pathos, and logos), and the three kinds of speech. The studies then follow in order: invention, arrangement, style, and whole cases drawn from actual speeches. The set of exercises does not grade compositions; it trains us to identify the kind of speech, the means of persuasion, the figures, and the missing premise of an enthymeme.

When to return

A first pass is finished when we can state the following in our own words:

  • What still has to be done for a particular audience after a sentence is correct and an argument is valid.
  • The three means of persuasion, and why ethos is in the speech rather than in the speaker’s reputation.
  • The three kinds of speech, and why they are named from what the hearer is asked to judge.
  • What makes someone a sophist.

With that, all three arts have been begun. The next page, The three arts together, sets them side by side and is meant to be consulted again whenever a difficulty arises.
