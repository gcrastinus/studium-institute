Go and practice rhetoric

❧

The preceding account said what this art is for; the set of exercises is where we practice it. Ars Rhetorica begins at Act I, What Rhetoric Is, with its opening tutorial, the card titled The counterpart of dialectic. That deck presents the definition, the three pisteis (the means of persuasion: ethos, pathos, and logos), and the three species (kinds of oratory), using real distinctions rather than invented classical-sounding prose. The studies then continue in the order of the set of exercises: invention (logos, then ethos and pathos), arrangement, style, and whole cases built from actual speeches.

The set of exercises does not grade compositions; it trains us to name the species, the pistis, the office of the oration, the figure that is actually in the English, and the missing premise of an enthymeme. We return to this course when we can say, in our own words, what still has to be done for these hearers after a sentence is well formed and an inference is valid, that is, its conclusion follows necessarily from its premises. A good test is the reverse of the green-cheese pair: a speech may be congruous (grammatically well formed) and true and still fail, if it has not found the available means in this case.

The place to begin is Act I, What Rhetoric Is, with the tutorial card The counterpart of dialectic, which is an orientation and is not scored. The address is rhetorica.studiuminstitute.org.

Open Ars Rhetorica practice

If we came from the voice path or from empty debate, we do this study, and then we return to grammar and logic, so that we have something true to persuade toward. If we came in the default order, a first pass of the orientation and at least the invention studies in the set of exercises come before figures, since figures without a case are only ornaments. The cycle is the same: doctrine, then practice, then doctrine again with better questions.

When we come back

The same test applies, in the third art’s terms. Without opening anything, the test is to say the following:

  • What is still to be done for these hearers after a sentence is well formed and an inference is valid.
  • The three pisteis, and why ethos is in the speech rather than in the speaker’s reputation.
  • The three species, named from the office of the hearer rather than from the subject.
  • What makes a man a sophist, and why it is not his faculty.

The harder test is the one the set of exercises trains for: we take a real speech that we did not choose, and we name its species, the pistis being used, and the missing premise of one of its enthymemes.

When we come back

All three arts have now been begun. That is what this introduction was for, and it is the point at which the introduction ceases to be the study underway and becomes something to consult.

The next account is The three arts together, which is written to be reread. It sets the three questions side by side and gives a plain order of questions for telling which art our present difficulty belongs to. From here on, we return by that account.

The cycle continues. To return to an art is not to have failed at it. When speech will not express what we meant, the trouble is in one of three places, and we now know how to find out which.
